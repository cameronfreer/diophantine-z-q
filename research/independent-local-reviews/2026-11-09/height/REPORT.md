## Height transfer from the actual three scales

The supplying round-eight input is the same qualified height estimate
`h(z)<=H*M(z)^alpha`, with `alpha=577/32` and

```
H=3*2^106*(2^32*3^9)^6*46410^18+128.
```

It applies to the actual beta: the anchor implies 2-integrality of X, so
`X=-1/2` and hence `beta=2401` are excluded; positivity excludes the other
finite exceptional parameters. The order-31 scalar result supplies
`M(beta)^31 | a-1`, yielding `h(beta)<=H*|a-1|^(577/992)`. This uses radical
order 31, not a stronger multiple of each odd contact valuation.

For the detectors the points, normalized contact functions and rational
scales remain `1,C0^2,U^2`, where `U=C0+C1` and
`C=C0+C1*sqrt(2)` is the four-root product in K. The positive root
abscissae at both embeddings give the inherited bounds `C0>=193`,
`U>=61`; these are the actual builder scales, not the different older
scale `(1+C1^2)^2`. Nonzero lambda and the contact exclusions make each
fractional-linear height application defined. The map
`z=-49+1/(A*lambda)` has inverse `A*lambda=1/(z+49)` and height cost at
most `log(50)`. Thus the supplied common bound is precisely
`T=(H+log(50))*|N|^(alpha/k)` for each nonzero consumed integer N.

The projective argument is independent of the exponent inside T. Over the
same normalized absolute heights, multiplication of a projective tuple by
nonzero lambda preserves height by the product formula; coordinatewise
squaring doubles height. At each place the maximum of three absolute
values is at most the product of their individual maxima with 1. Hence

```
2*h([1:C0:U])
 =h([lambda:C0^2*lambda:U^2*lambda]) <=3*T.
```

For `C=(1-sqrt(2))*C0+sqrt(2)*U`, the coefficients are integral at every
finite place. The two real operator bounds are `2*sqrt(2)-1` and
`2*sqrt(2)+1`; both exceed 1 and their product is 7. This gives
`h(C)<=3*T/2+log(7)/2`. In particular the real normalization is the mean
of the two logarithms, not their unnormalized sum.

The odd-product proof applies only after the anchor has established all
four labels odd. I checked its finite-place sign argument, its separate
place above seven and both real embeddings: it concerns the product C in
K and yields the sum of the root heights. Replacing C by its rational norm
would not be justified. The same root-height lower bound therefore gives

```
B^2-6 < K0*|N|^(alpha/k),  K0=(3H+14)/2.
```

Indeed `log(50)<4`, `log(7)<2` and `|N|^(alpha/k)>=1` bound the added
constants by 7. This derivation works for every positive k supplied by
the actual divisibility lemmas. It is a reapplication of the earlier
projective argument to the current T, not an unexplained replacement of
19 in the old theorem statement. The geometric/global proof of the
supplying H and alpha retains its existing qualifications.

## Uniform Lucas, scalar and input consumers

The actual residual is

comparisons give the three radical divisibilities at m=45 or 47, so the
new transfer applies to this same integer F.

The checked endpoint inequality is

```
[2*(b*^2-6)]^(32m) > (3H+14)^(32m)*P(b*)^577.
```

It is uniform beyond the endpoint: after extracting the powers of B, the
ratio has factor `B^(64m-2308)`, respectively exponents 572 and 700; the
positive factor `(1-6/B^2)^(32m)` increases for `B>sqrt(6)`, and the
positive denominator `(2+6/B+5/B^2+B^-4)^577` decreases there. The ratio
therefore strictly increases throughout the required domain. The exact
integer endpoint test is consequently sufficient for every allowed B,
not a finite sample offered as a uniform proof. It passes at (2118,45)
and (2010,47), forcing F=0. The unchanged finite nonidentity root supplies
`b!=0`, so the inherited Lucas conclusion is `log(B)>|a|-3`.

The independent integer tests confirm
`c*^415>(4H)^992` for the default route and
`8^992*c*^415>(25H)^992` for the optional route. Since
`1-alpha/31=415/992>0`, monotonicity extends them to every `c>=c*`.

the common latter bound.

For `n!=beta`, the actual nonzero reduced numerator N still satisfies

coarse input transfer applies. Absorbing the additive 6 uses B>1,
